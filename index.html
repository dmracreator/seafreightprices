Issue 39 · 29 September 2026 8 min read

Suez capacity returns just as Golden Week takes demand out

The two forces now setting Asia–Europe are pulling the same way. Containership transits through the canal keep climbing, and the holiday shutdown removes cargo for a fortnight. Carriers are blanking hard against it, and on the North Europe leg it is not proving enough.

  • Drewry's composite fell 1% to $4,468 per 40ft on 24 September, reversing the previous week's rise. Shanghai–Rotterdam dropped 4% to $3,485 and Shanghai–Genoa 5% to $3,835.
  • Suez containership transits rose to 48 in week 38 from 41 the week before. Drewry expects that returning capacity to outweigh the blanking programme on Asia–Europe, and rates to keep falling.
  • Gemini restored four more loops — AE5, AE11, AE12 and ME2 — on 14 September, with the 19,000 TEU Marchen Maersk first onto the rebuilt AE5 on the 21st. MSC, CMA CGM and COSCO have each moved selected services back; Evergreen, ONE, HMM and Yang Ming had not as of 25 September.
  • Transpacific is the exception, still firm: Shanghai–Los Angeles up 2% to $7,838, Shanghai–New York broadly flat at $10,373. Carriers announced 15 transpacific blank sailings for the coming week against nine the week before.
  • Mid-Autumn Festival ran 25–27 September and Golden Week runs 1–7 October, with three working days between them. Alliances have blanked roughly 13% of planned October capacity on Asia to US and Europe services so far — below the 18% and 21% withdrawn at the same point last year.
  • Worth an hour of someone's time: the US Federal Maritime Commission has confirmed there is no three-year limitation period on complaints about detention and demurrage charges assessed on or after 16 June 2022. Old invoices you wrote off may be back in scope.
  • Still grinding in the background — German labour disruption and low water on the Rhine on the European side, constrained transit capacity at Panama on the Atlantic one.

Index levels as published by Drewry for 24 September 2026; canal transit counts and blanking figures from Drewry's Container Capacity Insight. Service and regulatory items as reported by gCaptain, 16–24 September 2026.

Methodology

How the numbers are made.

A benchmark is only useful if you can explain it. This is the full basis of every rate, index and status published on SeaFreightPrices.com.

01

Collection

Rate submissions from forwarders, NVOCCs and shippers, combined with published carrier tariffs and booked-transaction data. Contributors submit under a fixed schema so like is compared with like.

02

Validation

Outliers beyond three median absolute deviations are quarantined and reviewed by hand. Any lane with fewer than eight independent contributors is published as indicative, and labelled as such.

03

Publication

Benchmarks are the trimmed mean of the validated sample, published at 08:00 CET each business day with contributor count, spread and revision history attached.

Independence

No carrier, terminal or forwarder holds an ownership stake in the index, and no commercial relationship affects a published number. Contributors can see their own submissions but never another party's. Where a lane is commercially connected to the wider ecosystem, it is disclosed on the lane page itself.
